# Use unified Itinerary Explore Panel

Landi will present attraction, dining, shopping, and local-experience Guide Cards in one Itinerary Explore Panel inside the itinerary workspace, including both material already used in the Recommended Itinerary and available alternatives. The panel keeps an All view for complete guide browsing and can be filtered by type, theme, area, and itinerary state, but those filters do not create separate top-level pages or day-owned candidate lists.

The Itinerary Explore Panel appears as one mode of the shared Workspace Side Panel. Opening a shared detail view switches that same side panel from explore mode to detail mode rather than opening a second sidebar.

When returning from detail mode, Web restores the prior explore filters, sort order, scroll position, and selected card through Side Panel Navigation State.

The panel, including its All view, defaults to Guide Relevance Order: relationship to the current trip, current day, or selected Itinerary Block comes before secondary popularity, distance, rating, freshness, image availability, or source-richness signals. When a day or block is selected, the All view follows that context; without a selected context, it falls back to trip-level relevance. The ranking serves plan adjustment rather than a generic destination chart, and the ranking reason is not shown on cards by default.

Cards in the panel are state-aware. Scheduled cards show an "in itinerary" state and open a Timeline-linked Guide Detail: Web locates and highlights the corresponding timeline block while the shared detail panel shows the Guide Item's image or text content, compact Source Marker, related guide material, and relevant alternatives. Relevant alternatives are shown in the shared detail panel after the traveler opens it, not on the timeline by default. Unscheduled cards with schedulable objects start itinerary edits. Informational-only cards remain searchable and filterable, can open details, and do not show scheduling or add actions.

Scattering backup material under individual days was rejected because unscheduled guide material should not imply a recommended day or time. A unified panel keeps the recommended timeline clean while still giving travelers one operational place to browse, compare, locate scheduled material, and start itinerary edits.
